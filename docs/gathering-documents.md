# Gathering Documents

Most benefit applications ask for the same kinds of paperwork. Collecting these documents before you start filling out forms will save you time and frustration.

## Common documents you may need

Not every program asks for everything on this list, but these are the most common:

- **Photo ID** — driver's license, state ID, passport, or another government-issued ID
- **Social Security number** — Social Security card, or a document showing your number (for you and household members)
- **Proof of income** — recent pay stubs, a letter from your employer, tax returns, or benefit award letters
- **Proof of address** — a utility bill, lease, or mail addressed to you at your current address
- **Birth certificates** — for you and any children or dependents in your household
- **Immigration documents** — if applicable (green card, work permit, visa, etc.)
- **Disability or medical records** — doctor's letters, diagnosis paperwork, or benefit award letters (for disability-related programs)
- **Bank statements** — sometimes needed to verify income or resources
- **Rent or mortgage documents** — lease, rent receipts, or mortgage statements (for housing programs)
- **Utility bills** — electric, gas, water, or internet bills (for utility assistance)

If you're applying for a specific program, check its website or call the office to confirm exactly which documents they need.

## Where to get missing documents

| Document | Where to request it |
|---|---|
| Birth certificate | Vital records office in the state where you were born |
| Social Security card | Social Security Administration (ssa.gov) |
| Photo ID / state ID | Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) |
| Tax return copy | IRS (irs.gov) or your tax preparer |
| Pay stubs | Your employer's HR or payroll office |
| Marriage or divorce certificate | Vital records office where the event was recorded |

Some replacements cost a fee and take weeks to arrive, so start early if you need to order anything.

## Organize as you go

- Make a folder (paper or digital) just for this application.
- Put a checklist on the front and check off each document as you collect it.
- Keep copies of everything you submit — never send your only copy.
- If you scan or photograph documents, save them with clear names like `2024-tax-return.pdf` or `pay-stub-june.pdf`.

## If you don't have a document

Don't give up on applying. Call the program office and ask:

- "Is there another document I can use instead?"
- "Can I apply now and provide this document later?"
- "Is there someone who can help me get this document?"

Many programs accept alternatives or let you submit documents after you apply.

## Tip for low energy days

Gathering documents is tiring. Do one document at a time, take breaks, and ask a friend or family member to help you make calls or copy paperwork. You don't have to do it all in one day.
