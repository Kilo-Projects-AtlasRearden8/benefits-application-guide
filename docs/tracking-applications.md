# Tracking Applications

After you submit, the waiting begins. This chapter helps you stay organized, follow up, and know what to do while you wait for a decision.

## Start a tracking sheet

Keep one page (paper or digital) for each application. Write down:

- Program name
- Date you applied
- How you applied (online, phone, mail, in person)
- Confirmation or reference number
- Office or caseworker name (if you have one)
- Phone number for questions
- Expected decision date (if given)
- Notes from phone calls or letters

Update it every time you do something related to the application.

## Save everything

- Confirmation emails, screenshots, or reference numbers
- Letters and notices you receive in the mail
- Notes from phone calls (date, who you spoke with, what they said)
- Copies of anything you mailed

Put them all in the same folder so nothing gets lost.

## Check your status

Many programs let you check your application status:

- **Online** — log into the account you used to apply
- **By phone** — call the office and have your reference number ready
- **In person** — visit the local office

Check once every week or two, or whenever the program tells you to. Checking too often won't speed things up, but staying aware helps you catch requests for more information quickly.

## Respond to requests quickly

The agency may ask for more documents or information. They'll usually send a letter, email, or call. Respond by the deadline they give you. If you need more time, call and ask — many offices will extend the deadline if you ask before it passes.

If you miss a deadline, your application could be delayed or denied. If that happens, call right away and ask what you can do.

## Common statuses and what they mean

| Status | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Received / pending | The agency has your application and is reviewing it |
| More information needed | They need a document or answer from you — send it soon |
| Approved | You qualify; watch for next steps (benefit start date, how to use it) |
| Denied | You don't qualify, or information is missing — you can usually appeal |

## If your application is denied

A denial isn't always the end. You usually have the right to **appeal** — ask the agency to review the decision again.

- Read the denial letter carefully. It should explain why and list the appeal deadline.
- Appeal before the deadline (often 30–90 days, but it varies).
- You can bring new documents or fix mistakes.
- Legal aid organizations sometimes help with appeals for free.

If you miss the appeal deadline, you can often reapply — but starting over takes longer, so appeal first if you can.

## What to expect while waiting

Processing times vary widely:

- Some programs decide in days or weeks
- Others take weeks or months (disability and housing programs often take longer)

If it's been longer than the time the agency estimated, call and politely check on the status.

## Low-energy tracking tips

- Set one day a week as "paperwork day" and spend just 15–20 minutes on it.
- Put tracking updates on your calendar so you don't forget.
- Ask a friend to check in with you once a week.
