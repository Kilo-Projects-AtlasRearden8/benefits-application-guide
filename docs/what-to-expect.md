# What to Expect

Knowing what happens after you apply can make the wait feel less uncertain. This chapter covers typical timelines, outcomes, and next steps.

## Timelines vary

Every program is different. Some common ranges:

- **Food assistance (SNAP)** — often decided within 30 days; faster in emergencies
- **Health coverage (Medicaid / CHIP)** — often 30–45 days, sometimes faster for pregnant people or children
- **Unemployment benefits** — often 2–3 weeks, sometimes longer
- **Disability benefits (SSI / SSDI)** — can take several months or longer
- **Housing assistance** — often months; many programs have waitlists

These are general examples, not promises. Your state or local office can tell you the expected time for the program you applied to.

## While you wait

- Check your status every 1–2 weeks (see [Tracking applications](tracking-applications.md)).
- Watch your mail and email for requests for more information.
- Respond quickly to requests — delays often come from missing documents.
- Keep your tracking sheet updated.

## Possible outcomes

### Approved

You'll get a notice explaining:

- What benefits you qualify for
- When they start
- How much you'll receive (if applicable)
- How to use them (EBT card, insurance card, direct deposit, etc.)
- Any steps you need to take next

Keep the approval letter. You may need it later.

### Denied

The notice should explain why. Common reasons include:

- Income above the limit
- Missing documents or information
- Not meeting a specific eligibility rule

You can usually appeal. Read the denial letter for the deadline and steps. Legal aid organizations sometimes help with appeals at no cost.

### Request for more information

This isn't a denial — the agency needs something from you. Send it by the deadline, or call and ask for more time if you need it.

## After approval: using your benefits

Benefits arrive in different ways:

- **EBT card** — for food assistance; works like a debit card at participating stores
- **Insurance card** — for health coverage; use it when you get care
- **Direct deposit** — cash benefits go into your bank account
- **Vendor payments** — some housing or utility help is paid directly to your landlord or utility company

Some benefits require you to recertify (reapply or update your information) every 6–12 months. Put the recertification date on your calendar so you don't lose coverage.

## If your situation changes

Tell the agency if:

- Your income changes
- Someone moves in or out of your household
- You move to a new address
- Your work hours change

Changes can affect your benefits. Reporting them early prevents overpayments you might have to pay back.

## Help along the way

You don't have to figure this out alone:

- **Program office** — call with questions about your application
- **Legal aid** — free or low-cost help with denials and appeals
- **Community action agencies** — help with applications and follow-up
- **Libraries** — free computer access and research help
- **Trusted friends or family** — someone to sit with you while you work

## Education only

This guide explains typical processes in plain language. It is not legal advice and does not guarantee any outcome. Confirm requirements and timelines with the program or agency directly.
