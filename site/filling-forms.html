3. Filling Out Forms

Forms are where most people get stuck. Work through an application one step at a time so it feels manageable instead of overwhelming.

Education only, not legal advice. Form requirements vary by program. Confirm instructions with the program or agency directly.

Before you start

  • Set aside a block of time when you won’t be interrupted. Even 30 minutes helps.
  • Have your gathered documents nearby.
  • Have a pen and paper (or a notes app) for writing down questions.
  • It’s okay to stop and come back later. Most applications let you save your progress or return to a paper form.

Ways to apply

Most programs offer more than one way to apply. Choose what works best for you:

  • Online — fastest for many programs; you can save and return later
  • By phone — good if you want someone to walk you through it
  • In person — at a local office; helpful if you need hands-on help
  • By mail — paper forms you print, fill out, and send
  • With help — a caseworker, librarian, or trusted friend can assist you

If one way doesn’t work for you, try another.

1

Read the whole form first

Skim the entire application before writing anything. Note which sections exist, what documents you need, and where you need to sign.

2

Fill in your personal information

Start with your name, address, phone number, and date of birth. Write exactly as it appears on your ID.

3

Answer household questions

You’ll likely be asked who lives with you, their ages, and their income. Include everyone the form asks for — leaving people out can cause delays.

4

Answer income and expense questions

Use your pay stubs, tax returns, or benefit letters. If a question asks for monthly income and you’re paid weekly, do the math or write “paid weekly” next to the answer so the reviewer knows.

5

Attach or upload documents

Follow the program’s instructions for submitting documents. Online applications usually let you upload photos or scans. Paper applications may ask for copies (not originals).

6

Review before submitting

Double-check: names are spelled correctly, Social Security numbers are correct, income amounts match your documents, and you’ve signed and dated the form.

7

Submit and save confirmation

If applying online, save or screenshot the confirmation page and note the confirmation number. If applying by mail, make a copy of the whole package and consider sending it with tracking or certified mail.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving blank questions (write “none” or “not applicable” if a question doesn’t apply to you)
  • Guessing income amounts instead of checking your documents
  • Forgetting to sign and date
  • Sending original documents instead of copies
  • Missing deadlines

If you get stuck

  • Skip and come back. Mark the question with a note and move on.
  • Call the program office. Ask them to explain the question.
  • Ask a helper. A caseworker, librarian, or trusted friend can sit with you.
  • Take a break. Come back when you have more energy.

If you need accommodations

Programs must provide reasonable accommodations for disabilities. You can ask for:

  • Help filling out the form
  • Extra time
  • Forms in large print, braille, or another language
  • An interpreter

Don’t be afraid to ask — it’s your right.

After you submit, write down: the date you submitted, how you submitted it, any confirmation or reference number, and who you spoke with (if you talked to anyone).
