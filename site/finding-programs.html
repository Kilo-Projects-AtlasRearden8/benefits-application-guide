1. Finding Programs

The first step is figuring out which benefits and assistance programs you might qualify for.

Education only, not legal advice. Program rules, eligibility, and application steps vary by location and change over time. Confirm current requirements with the program or agency directly before applying.

Start with what you need

Benefits programs usually support specific needs. Think about your situation and make a short list. Common categories include:

  • Food and nutrition — help buying groceries or meals
  • Healthcare — health insurance, medical bills, prescriptions
  • Housing — rent help, utilities, homelessness prevention
  • Income support — cash assistance, unemployment, disability
  • Family and children — childcare, school meals, family leave
  • Seniors and people with disabilities — long-term care, in-home support
  • Veterans and military families — VA benefits, education, housing
  • Emergency help — disaster relief, crisis assistance, burial assistance

You don’t need to fit into just one category. Many people qualify for several programs at once.

Where to look

Official government websites (start here)

  • Benefits.gov — official U.S. government benefits finder. Answer a short questionnaire to see which federal programs you might qualify for.
  • USA.gov — the official U.S. government website; good for finding agencies and offices.
  • State government websites — search “[your state] benefits” or “[your state] health and human services.”
  • County or city websites — local offices often run rental assistance, utility aid, and food programs.

Other trustworthy places

  • 211.org — dial 211 or visit 211.org to connect with local community resources (food, housing, mental health, and more).
  • Local libraries — librarians can help you search and often provide free computer access.
  • Community action agencies — nonprofits that help low-income residents find and apply for benefits.
  • Faith-based groups and shelters — often know about local emergency assistance programs.

Programs you may already know

Some widely available programs include:

  • SNAP — food assistance (formerly food stamps)
  • WIC — nutrition support for pregnant people and young children
  • Medicaid / CHIP — health coverage for low-income adults and children
  • Unemployment insurance — temporary income after job loss
  • SSI / SSDI — disability income programs (Social Security)
  • LIHEAP — home energy bill assistance
  • Section 8 / housing vouchers — rental assistance
  • Free and reduced-price school meals — for children in school
  • Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) — tax credit for low- to moderate-income workers

This is not a complete list, and availability varies by location.

How to check eligibility

Most programs have eligibility rules based on:

  • Income (how much you earn, often compared to the federal poverty level)
  • Household size (how many people live with you)
  • Residency (where you live)
  • Age, disability status, pregnancy, or citizenship/immigration status
  • Employment or recent job loss

You usually don’t need to know all the rules before applying. Many applications will tell you whether you qualify as part of the process. If a website has an “am I eligible” or “pre-screener” tool, that’s a good place to start.
